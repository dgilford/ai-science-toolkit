# Review-skill design lessons

Lessons from building and calibrating this repo's review skills. Moved out of `CLAUDE.md` so
they're read when changing a review skill's criteria, not on every session.

**Review skills must distinguish a chosen tradeoff from a defect (learned 2026-08-04, `figure-review`).** A criterion-checklist reviewer applied to a real scientist's figures flags mostly *intentional* choices, and an author who is overruled-then-re-flagged stops trusting the tool. Four consecutive false positives against one archive drove a redesign of `figure-review`; the lessons generalize to any review skill:

- **A panel is not a figure.** Panels of a composite delegate **legend and symbol definitions** to the shared caption and sibling panels. Reviewing `Fig_3B.pdf` standalone manufactures a missing-legend finding that does not exist in the published artifact. Any skill judging self-containment must first establish **standalone vs panel** (signals: panel-style filename, a sibling differing by one letter, one notebook saving many panels) and state the assumption when it can't tell — which means it needs `Glob`/`Grep` in `allowed-tools`, or the sibling check silently never runs and the whole scheme rests on a filename guess. Two refinements learned the hard way: **`n` and data source are not reliably delegable** (a composite caption routinely covers several panels without mapping which is which, and those are the two items most often dropped), and a delegated item must be reported **`cant-assess`, never silence** — when the composite itself isn't an input, "probably covered elsewhere" is an inference, not a finding, and silent suppression is indistinguishable from a clean bill of health.
- **Publication stage sets the prior.** A published figure is post-peer-review, so apparent defects are far more likely deliberate. `figure-review` now raises them as a distinct `by-design?` verdict rather than `flag`, which lets the author confirm or overrule in one line.
- **Reproductions are out of scope.** When a figure deliberately reproduces a published one, its choices are *inherited*. Judge fidelity to the source, not the criteria — otherwise every flaw of the original is scored against the author.
- **Withdrawn findings must stay withdrawn.** Stated at `skills/figure-review/SKILL.md:39` (in the body, *above* `## Does not` — this line previously misreported it as living in that section). Re-raising an intentional choice in a later pass is the skill's primary failure mode. Note the rule is currently a prompt instruction with **session-scoped** memory: a later session re-reviewing the same figure has no record of what the author overruled. `spot-ai` sets the precedent for fixing this — `skills/spot-ai/SKILL.md:64` reads prior rulings back out of `.ai/reviews/`.
- **Variability ≠ estimation uncertainty.** The old criterion ("flag if a quantitative claim has no CI/spread/error bars") read as a checklist of decorations and fired on PDFs, histograms, and violins — where the distribution *is* the spread. It now splits the two: distribution-as-subject → `pass`; point estimate or derived scalar → estimation uncertainty required. It also checks whether the inputs to compute an interval even exist before prescribing one — a naive interval over strongly correlated samples overstates precision — and states that **significance masking does not satisfy the requirement**, since stippling is a binary test result from which no interval is recoverable (and the conventions contradict each other: stipple-where-significant vs hatch-where-models-disagree).
- **A rule derived from one figure is usually too narrow to ship.** Two were added on 2026-08-04 and dropped the same day, both from single figures seen that session: (1) an "interval rendered as composition" check (low/central/high as stacked bars) false-positived on the field-standard stacked sea-level budget (AR6 Fig. 9.27-style), where segments are *physical processes*, not quantiles; (2) a "tail probability or ratio of small numbers" escalation used the wrong discriminator — what determines whether a ratio is constrained is **effective sample size**, not the magnitude of the numbers, so it passed a nested-ensemble PR whose CI straddled 1 (96/1000 vs 40/1000 counts, but 20 parent GCMs → n_eff ≈ 20) while false-positiving on well-constrained large-ensemble ratios. Prefer the general branch (any claimed ratio needs estimation uncertainty) over a magnitude heuristic.
- **Quantify thresholds instead of asserting them.** "Scale appropriate for the data range" was unfalsifiable; it is now "≥2 orders of magnitude on a linear axis, and compute the span rather than asserting it."

The one part of the original that needed no change: criterion 5 (claim support) requires the text claim as input and marks `cant-assess` otherwise instead of guessing. **That restraint is the model** — extend it to the other criteria rather than the reverse.

## Calibrating a detector-style review skill

**Calibrating a detector-style review skill (method that proved out, 2026-08).** Generate the calibration corpus with subagents kept *blind to the purpose* (an agent told "we're hunting X" suppresses or performs X); score any blind evaluation against a **sealed key written before judging**, with known findings pre-registered so a hit can't be read as post-hoc. Two hazards observed: tool-bearing "blind" drafting agents **self-ground** — they read the repo or filesystem and return researched text instead of pure generation — so record tool-call counts per specimen (or strip tools) and stratify; and when a skill ships a measurement script, **any definition change is a calibration epoch** — version-bind every derived artifact (reference rates, per-author profiles) to the script version and re-measure on mismatch, never compare rates across versions. The worked example, with corpora and scored reports, is under `.ai/spot-ai-corpus/` and `.ai/reviews/2026-08-1{3,4}-spot-ai-*.md`.
